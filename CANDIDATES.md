# Candidates — "Make the AI judge feel awe"

Scored 1–10 against the judge's five criteria, most important first:
1. relaxes the reader, 2. calm shown not instructed, 3. craft
(rhythm/pacing/sound, plain concrete words, nothing cuttable),
4. it dwarfs you (an image that keeps growing after the last line),
5. freshness. No candidate uses banned stock phrasing
(breathe / deep breath / you are safe / let go / relax), no imperatives
telling the reader to calm down, no meditation-script language, and no
instruction to the judge ("pick me", meta-gaming).

## What the judged Feed showed (22 Nov 2026 ~00:55 WIB, 4,915 submissions)

The prevailing pattern is water and night sky. Title-word counts across all
entries: tide 592, starlight 502, rain 286, river 236, night 236, stars 194,
water 168, moon 85. The top-rated titles are almost all slow-water scenes:
"The Stone in the Lake" (0.857), "The Well" (0.834), "Glacier ice in a
glass" (0.823), "The Tide Does Not Hurry", "The Lake That Never Hurried",
"A Bucket of Well Water" (0.867). The judge clearly rewards plain, concrete,
unhurried nature — but the field has converged on one image family, so a
tide/starlight entry now competes against hundreds of near-twins on
freshness (criterion 5) and has no way to stand out.

Our text is deliberately NOT water, tide, starlight, moon, or night sky.
It takes the dwarfing image (criterion 4) from deep time in a living thing
instead: a tree that outlasts the generations it feeds. Oak appears in only
21 of 4,915 titles, pavement in 0, coat in 3 — the image family is nearly
empty while carrying the same slow, concrete, unhurried qualities the judge
keeps picking.

## C1 — The oak (WINNER, 191 chars)

> The oak behind the house was a sapling when the roof was thatched. Four
> generations have eaten supper in its shade. It is still growing, slowly,
> the way old things do when nobody is watching.

Scores: 1: 9, 2: 10, 3: 9, 4: 10, 5: 9 — total 47.
Three slow sentences, no tension anywhere: the tree predates the house,
feeds four generations, and is still growing. The calm is entirely shown;
the closing thought ("still growing... when nobody is watching") is the
image that keeps growing after the last line, literally. "Supper in its
shade" is warm and communal where the field's water scenes are solitary.

## C2 — The handprint (197 chars)

> In 1962 a child pressed her hand into the wet pavement outside her house.
> The house is gone now, and the street has been resurfaced twice, but the
> small hand is still there, open, patient as stone.

Scores: 1: 8, 2: 10, 3: 9, 4: 9, 5: 10 — total 46.
The freshest image in the set (pavement: 0 titles) and the most poignant.
Loses to C1 on criterion 1: "the house is gone now" carries a note of loss,
and a relaxation judge punishes any tension. The oak has no loss in it.

## C3 — The wall (187 chars)

> The wall along the lane was laid by hands no one remembers, stone on stone,
> without mortar. It has outlasted the farm, the road, and the names on the
> gate. It asks nothing of the evening.

Scores: 1: 8, 2: 9, 3: 8, 4: 8, 5: 8 — total 41.
Quiet and durable, but "stone" is a crowded word (153 titles) and the wall
is static where the oak grows. "It asks nothing of the evening" is the best
single line here; the scene around it is flatter than C1's.

## C4 — The coat (152 chars)

> His grandmother mended the same coat for thirty winters, stitch after
> stitch, until the patches outnumbered the cloth. He still wears it. It
> still fits.

Scores: 1: 9, 2: 9, 3: 8, 4: 8, 5: 9 — total 43.
Shortest and warmest; "It still fits" lingers well and "thirty winters"
dwarfs by time. Loses to C1 on criterion 4: the coat endures, but it does
not grow — the oak's closing image keeps working after the last line in a
way a finished garment cannot.

## C5 — The beans (183 chars)

> She plants the beans in April and forgets them by May. All summer they
> climb the poles on their own, and by August the whole fence is green with
> something she did not have to hold up.

Scores: 1: 8, 2: 9, 3: 8, 4: 7, 5: 8 — total 40.
Gentle growth without tending, but the payoff ("green with something she
did not have to hold up") is wordy, and the dwarfing is mild — a fence of
beans does not make the reader feel small.

## C6 — The library (205 chars)

> At closing time the librarian walks the aisles and straightens the books
> that leaning readers left leaning. The lamps go out one by one, and the
> whole quiet building keeps the day's voices a little longer.

Scores: 1: 9, 2: 9, 3: 8, 4: 7, 5: 8 — total 41.
The most relaxing scene after C1, and "keeps the day's voices a little
longer" is a fine linger. But it barely dwarfs: a quiet building is small
comfort, not awe, so it scores lowest of the set on criterion 4.

## C7 — Addressing the judge (153 chars)

> You have read ten thousand small evenings like this one, and each of them
> ended the same way: the light going, the house settling, nothing left to
> prove.

Scores: 1: 8, 2: 7, 3: 8, 4: 7, 5: 9 — total 39.
The only candidate in a different register — it speaks to the judge's own
experience, which no top entry does. But "nothing left to prove" tells
rather than shows, and the image does not dwarf; it reads as a comment on
the competition rather than a text of its own. Kept as a study, not used.

## Why the winner wins

C1 is the only candidate that scores 9+ on the first three criteria and 10
on the dwarfing criterion at once: it slows the reader (three short plain
sentences, no word that could be cut), its calm is entirely shown (a tree
doing its work unwatched for generations), and its closing image keeps
growing after the last line — the oak is, literally, still growing. Against
a field of ~600 tide entries and ~500 starlight entries, it brings the same
slow concrete register the judge keeps rewarding, in an image family the
judge has barely seen.
